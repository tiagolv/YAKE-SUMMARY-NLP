Reinforcement learning enables autonomous agents to learn optimal control policies through trial-and-error interaction with their environment, making it particularly suitable for robotic manipulation and locomotion tasks. Model-free methods such as proximal policy optimization and soft actor-critic have achieved impressive results in simulated environments, but transferring these policies to physical robots remains challenging due to the simulation-to-reality gap. Domain randomization and system identification techniques help bridge this gap by exposing agents to diverse simulation conditions during training. Reward shaping and curriculum learning strategies accelerate convergence by providing intermediate learning signals and gradually increasing task difficulty. Multi-agent reinforcement learning extends these concepts to cooperative and competitive scenarios where multiple robots must coordinate their actions. Sim-to-real transfer has been successfully demonstrated in dexterous manipulation tasks, quadruped locomotion, and drone navigation. Safety constraints are critical in real-world deployment, motivating research into constrained optimization formulations and safe exploration strategies. Offline reinforcement learning methods that learn from previously collected datasets without additional environment interaction offer a promising direction for applications where online data collection is expensive or dangerous. Hierarchical reinforcement learning decomposes complex tasks into subtask sequences, improving sample efficiency and enabling transfer across related tasks.
