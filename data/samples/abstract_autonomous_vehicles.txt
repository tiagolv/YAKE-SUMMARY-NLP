Perception systems for autonomous vehicles must reliably detect, classify, and track objects in complex driving environments under varying weather and lighting conditions. LiDAR point cloud processing with architectures such as PointNet and VoxelNet enables accurate three-dimensional object detection, while camera-based methods using monocular and stereo vision provide dense semantic information at lower hardware cost. Sensor fusion approaches that combine LiDAR, camera, and radar data exploit the complementary strengths of each modality to improve detection robustness and reduce failure modes. Bird's-eye view representations have emerged as a unified framework for multi-sensor fusion, projecting heterogeneous sensor data into a common spatial reference frame. Temporal modeling through recurrent networks and attention mechanisms enables object tracking and trajectory prediction, which are essential for safe motion planning. Occupancy networks provide a general representation of the driving scene, predicting which regions of space are occupied regardless of object category. Adversarial weather conditions including rain, fog, and snow degrade sensor performance, motivating research into domain adaptation and robust perception algorithms. The annotation of three-dimensional point clouds is expensive and time-consuming, driving interest in self-supervised and weakly-supervised learning methods that reduce the dependency on large labeled datasets.
