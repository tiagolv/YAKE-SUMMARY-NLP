Recommender systems play a central role in modern digital platforms by personalizing content delivery and reducing information overload for users. Collaborative filtering methods exploit user-item interaction patterns to predict preferences, while content-based approaches leverage item features and user profiles to generate recommendations. Matrix factorization techniques, including singular value decomposition and non-negative matrix factorization, remain competitive baselines that capture latent factors underlying user preferences. Deep learning models such as neural collaborative filtering and autoencoders have expanded the modeling capacity of recommender systems, enabling them to incorporate side information including text reviews, images, and social network connections. Graph neural networks model the complex relational structure between users and items, propagating information through interaction graphs to learn enriched representations. Sequential recommendation models based on transformers capture temporal dynamics in user behavior, predicting next items of interest based on browsing and purchase history. Cold-start problems arise when new users or items lack sufficient interaction data, motivating hybrid approaches that combine collaborative and content signals. Fairness and diversity in recommendations have gained increasing attention, with researchers developing algorithms that balance relevance with exposure fairness across item providers and demographic groups.
