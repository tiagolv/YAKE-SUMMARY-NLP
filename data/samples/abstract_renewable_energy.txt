Accurate forecasting of renewable energy generation is critical for integrating solar and wind power into electricity grids while maintaining supply-demand balance and grid stability. Statistical methods such as autoregressive integrated moving average models have been widely used for short-term forecasting, but their linear assumptions limit performance under rapidly changing weather conditions. Machine learning approaches, including gradient boosting, random forests, and deep neural networks, capture nonlinear relationships between meteorological variables and power output, consistently outperforming traditional statistical models. Hybrid architectures that combine convolutional layers for spatial feature extraction with recurrent layers for temporal modeling have shown particular promise for multi-step ahead predictions. Numerical weather prediction models provide valuable input features, but their coarse spatial resolution requires statistical post-processing to align with specific generation sites. Probabilistic forecasting methods quantify prediction uncertainty, enabling grid operators to make risk-aware decisions about reserve allocation and market participation. Energy storage systems, including batteries and pumped hydro, serve as buffers against forecast errors, and their optimal scheduling depends on accurate probabilistic forecasts. The integration of distributed energy resources and the emergence of prosumer communities create new challenges for aggregated forecasting at different spatial scales.
