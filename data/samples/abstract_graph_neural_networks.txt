Graph neural networks extend deep learning to non-Euclidean domains by operating directly on graph-structured data, enabling applications in social network analysis, molecular property prediction, and knowledge graph reasoning. Message passing frameworks aggregate information from neighboring nodes through learned transformations, iteratively refining node representations to capture both local and global graph structure. Graph attention networks introduce learnable attention weights that allow each node to selectively attend to relevant neighbors, improving representation quality on heterogeneous graphs. Spectral methods based on graph Fourier transforms provide a principled mathematical foundation but are limited to fixed graph topologies. Over-smoothing, where node representations become indistinguishable as network depth increases, remains a fundamental challenge that limits the expressiveness of deep graph networks. Graph generation models based on variational autoencoders and autoregressive approaches enable the synthesis of novel molecular structures with desired chemical properties, accelerating drug discovery pipelines. Scalability is a practical concern for graphs with millions of nodes and edges, motivating mini-batch training strategies, graph sampling, and distributed computation approaches. Temporal graph networks capture evolving interactions by incorporating time-stamped edges, supporting applications such as fraud detection and dynamic social network analysis.
