Sentiment analysis of social media text presents unique challenges due to the informal language, abbreviations, emojis, and sarcasm prevalent in user-generated content. Traditional approaches based on lexicon matching and bag-of-words features capture surface-level sentiment cues but fail to model contextual nuances and negation patterns. Pre-trained language models such as BERT and RoBERTa achieve superior performance by learning deep bidirectional representations that encode syntactic and semantic information from large text corpora. Aspect-based sentiment analysis extends the task beyond document-level polarity classification by identifying sentiment toward specific entities or features mentioned in the text, enabling more actionable insights for businesses and policymakers. Multilingual sentiment analysis addresses the need to process content in multiple languages, leveraging cross-lingual transfer learning and multilingual pre-trained models like XLM-RoBERTa. Multimodal sentiment analysis combines textual content with visual and acoustic signals from videos and images, capturing sentiment cues that text alone cannot convey. Domain adaptation remains essential as sentiment expressions vary significantly across product reviews, political discourse, and healthcare discussions. Explainable sentiment analysis methods provide justifications for predictions, highlighting which words or phrases most strongly influence the model output and building user trust in automated systems.
