Federated learning enables collaborative model training across decentralized data sources without requiring raw data to leave individual devices or institutions, addressing privacy concerns and data governance regulations. The canonical federated averaging algorithm aggregates locally trained model updates from participating clients, but communication efficiency remains a bottleneck when thousands of devices contribute to training over bandwidth-constrained networks. Non-independent and identically distributed data across clients, known as statistical heterogeneity, degrades model convergence and performance, motivating personalized federated learning approaches that adapt global models to local data distributions. Differential privacy mechanisms add calibrated noise to model updates before aggregation, providing formal privacy guarantees against inference attacks. Secure aggregation protocols based on cryptographic techniques ensure that the central server cannot inspect individual client contributions. Byzantine-robust aggregation methods defend against malicious participants that submit corrupted updates to manipulate the global model. Cross-device settings involving millions of mobile phones introduce additional challenges related to client availability, stragglers, and limited computational resources. Cross-silo federated learning between hospitals and financial institutions has demonstrated practical value for applications where data sharing is legally restricted. Vertical federated learning handles scenarios where different parties hold different features for overlapping populations, enabling collaborative model training without exposing proprietary datasets.
