Machine learning techniques are increasingly applied to climate science for improving weather prediction, detecting extreme events, and modeling complex Earth system processes. Convolutional neural networks have shown promise in downscaling climate projections from coarse global models to regional scales, while recurrent architectures capture temporal dynamics in atmospheric and oceanic data. Ensemble methods combining multiple models provide uncertainty quantification, which is critical for policy-relevant climate assessments. Recent advances in physics-informed neural networks integrate physical constraints directly into the learning process, ensuring that predictions respect fundamental conservation laws. Remote sensing data from satellite observations provide vast training datasets, enabling deep learning models to monitor deforestation, track glacier retreat, and estimate carbon fluxes at unprecedented spatial and temporal resolution. Transfer learning approaches have proven effective when labeled climate data is scarce, allowing models trained on reanalysis products to generalize to observational records. Challenges remain in handling the non-stationarity of climate systems, accounting for feedback loops, and ensuring that model predictions remain reliable under novel climate conditions not represented in historical training data. Interpretable machine learning methods are gaining attention for their ability to reveal physically meaningful patterns and build trust among domain scientists.
