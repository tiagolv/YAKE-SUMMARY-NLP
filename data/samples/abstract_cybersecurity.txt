Network intrusion detection systems are essential for protecting modern computing infrastructures against increasingly sophisticated cyber attacks. Traditional signature-based approaches rely on known attack patterns and fail to detect novel threats, motivating the adoption of machine learning methods for anomaly detection. Deep learning architectures, including autoencoders and generative adversarial networks, have demonstrated strong performance in identifying previously unseen attack vectors by learning representations of normal network behavior. The growing adoption of Internet of Things devices introduces additional security challenges due to resource constraints, heterogeneous protocols, and expanded attack surfaces. Federated learning has emerged as a privacy-preserving approach to collaborative intrusion detection, allowing multiple organizations to jointly train detection models without sharing sensitive network traffic data. Adversarial machine learning poses a significant concern, as attackers can craft inputs specifically designed to evade detection models. Recent research has explored robust training techniques and adversarial example detection to mitigate these threats. The evaluation of intrusion detection systems remains challenging due to the lack of up-to-date, realistic benchmark datasets that accurately represent modern network environments. Class imbalance between normal traffic and attack instances further complicates model training and evaluation, requiring specialized sampling strategies and evaluation metrics.
