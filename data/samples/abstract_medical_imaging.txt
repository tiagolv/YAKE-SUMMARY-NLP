Deep learning has transformed medical image analysis by enabling automated detection, segmentation, and classification of pathological findings across multiple imaging modalities. Convolutional neural networks trained on large annotated datasets achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to experienced radiologists in tasks such as detecting diabetic retinopathy from fundus photographs, identifying pulmonary nodules in chest computed tomography scans, and classifying skin lesions from dermoscopic images. Data augmentation techniques and transfer learning from natural image datasets help address the limited availability of labeled medical images, which require expensive expert annotation. U-Net and its variants have become the standard architecture for medical image segmentation, providing precise delineation of organs and tumors that supports surgical planning and radiation therapy. Attention mechanisms and multi-scale feature fusion improve model sensitivity to small lesions that might otherwise be overlooked. Federated learning approaches allow hospitals to collaboratively train models while maintaining patient data privacy in compliance with regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR. Despite impressive results on curated benchmark datasets, challenges persist in deploying these systems in clinical practice, including domain shift between training and deployment settings, lack of model interpretability, and the need for rigorous prospective validation studies.
